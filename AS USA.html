She had no one to inherit her farm - Then a young family’s dream changed everything “We had this connection with the place”

Greg Heilman

Farming is a tough life, long days full of chores to keep things growing and healthy so that operation runs smoothly. But for some they just feel that it’s a calling like Sarah and Jason Ricks, who dreamed of having their own on which to raise their kids.

However, they kept running into a common problem for new farmers, especially nowadays, finding affordable land. The problem is nationwide but for a feel of the scale, in Ohio where the Ricks were looking for land, the price has risen from $3,180 per acre two decades ago to $9,350 per acre reports Farm and Dairy. In just one year alone, cropland rose by 9.7% between 2023 and 2024.

“It just felt like I was helping to create a bit of a revolution in agriculture”

The couple had a stroke of luck though when they met Kristina Villa, co-executive director and co-founder of the Farmers Land Trust, a non-profit organization that helps farmers transition their farmland through a national land trust to the next generation by establishing community-managed farmland. She suggested that they apply to become the new farmers of a farm in Muskingum County that Farmers Land Trust had acquired from Annie Warmke.

Warmke had fallen and broke her leg in 2023 that had left her unable to continue farming the 38-acre piece of land with her husband, much less able to walk. She couldn’t find anybody to take over her Blue Rock Station farm, but she wanted to make sure it continued to be used for agriculture and didn’t become a development.

Using the Farmers Land Trust was the perfect way to transition her farm to the next generation for her. “When I realized they could come without debt, that meant that was an entry for possibly intergenerational wealth if they came with children. It just felt like I was helping to create a bit of a revolution in agriculture,” Warmke told Farm and Dairy.

It was also a good fit for the Ricks. Sarah said that not only did the organization provide access to land, but also that it selected people to who would use regenerative farming practices. Furthermore, the Farmer Land Trust gives the chosen farmers a 99-year lease meaning the Ricks wouldn’t have to worry about a mortgage taking pressure off of them to immediately begin producing food. Instead they could focus on making the land more fertile.

Additionally, thanks to the farming practices of the Warmkes on Blue Rock Station, it was also a perfect fit. “We had this connection with the place; we couldn’t deny it,” Jason recalled after visiting the farm for the first time.

“This process is a dream come true, because what it does is it protects your hard work,” Warmke reflected. “It gives you a sense of an insurance policy.”
